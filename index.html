CHAPTER I

The Clockmaker's Last Hour

CLOCK

The rain had been falling over London for three days without pause, as though the city had been placed beneath a vast grey curtain and forgotten there. By the third evening, the streets had become rivers of reflected lamplight, the gutters were swollen with black water, and every passing carriage seemed to emerge from the mist only to vanish into it again. Detective Elias Vale had spent most of the day indoors, which was unusual for a man who disliked being still. He sat in the narrow room above his office on Bell Street, surrounded by books, newspapers, unfinished notes, and the remains of a fire that had surrendered hours earlier.

A sudden knock disturbed the silence. Three sharp sounds, followed by nothing. Vale descended the narrow staircase and opened the front door to find a boy standing in the rain, his coat soaked and his expression frightened. The boy held out an envelope. This is for you, he said. The paper was perfectly dry, though everything around him was wet. On its front was a circle containing the number 17.

Inside were four words: THE CLOCK HAS STOPPED. On the back was a name Elias Vale had not heard in seventeen years: SILAS WREN.

Silas Wren's shop stood near the river in a part of London that seemed forgotten by the rest of the city. When Vale arrived, Inspector Thomas Rowan was already there. Hundreds of clocks covered the walls, but every one of them had stopped. At the centre of the room sat Silas Wren, dead in his chair. The doctor suspected poison. There was no sign of a struggle.

Vale examined Wren's right hand. It remained partly closed, deliberately so. Inside was a tiny brass key engraved with the number 17. Moments later, a hidden compartment beneath the floor opened with a mechanical click. Inside stood a black wooden box marked: FOR ELIAS VALE.

The box contained a black leather notebook. On its first page were the words: TO THE DETECTIVE WHO STILL BELIEVES THE PAST IS DEAD. Beneath them was the same circle and the number 17. Somewhere in London, someone had begun counting backward.
